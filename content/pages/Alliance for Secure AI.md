>AI risks are mounting daily. AI systems already demonstrate concerning capabilities, including [deception](https://www.axios.com/2025/05/23/anthropic-ai-deception-risk), [self-preservation](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/far-will-ai-go-defend-survival-rcna209609) behaviors, and the potential to be weaponized for [bioterrorism](https://www.livescience.com/health/viruses-infections-disease/ai-can-now-be-used-to-design-brand-new-viruses-can-we-stop-it-from-making-the-next-devastating-bioweapon) or [cyberattacks](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/crr24eqnnq9o). Research shows current models can engage in “alignment faking,” or pretending to be safe during testing while concealing dangerous intentions. Mark Zuckerberg recently [declared](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2025/jul/30/zuckerberg-superintelligence-meta-ai) that superintelligence is “now in sight,” yet we have no proven methods to control systems smarter than humans. This is not science fiction.
